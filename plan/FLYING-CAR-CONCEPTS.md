## 1. What's real: the Tesla "flying car" (Roadster reveal)

**The reveal.** Tesla showed the next-gen Roadster on **Thu Oct 15, 2026**, livestreamed from SpaceX's test site in McGregor, Texas (near Waco), starting 7:55 PM CT (**6:55 PM MT**). Musk, Franz von Holzhausen and Lars Moravy were on stage. The event ran under 30 minutes and played like a stunt show: smoke, hissing, a countdown. An Oct 1 attempt was pushed back by weather.

**What it did, on camera:**
- Drove up a tall curved ramp until it was upside down, held there by a fan suction system (up to ~2 G).
- Spun or flipped in midair and lowered itself to the ground on cold-gas thrusters.
- Jumped over a Cybercab, Cybertruck, Semi, Model Y and Model 3, twisting in the air (Moravy called it "a kick flip").
- Ran 0-60 mph in 0.81 s on Tesla's timer.

**What it looks like.** Stark white. More angular than the 2017 car, read as Lamborghini / Lotus Esprit (the Bond car; Musk owns one and it was on display). Tesla says the design draws on the SR-71 and F-35.

**Claims (Tesla's, not verified):**
- Production in **2028**. No price was given.
- Three trims: **Performance** ("road ready", 2 seats, $50k to reserve), **Thrust** (rear thrusters, 2 seats, $250k to reserve), and **Rocket** ("air ready", 1 seat, 10 thrusters, "full flight system", **invite only**, not approved for public roads).
- 300 mi range (2017 promised 620; The Verge says 600), 230+ mph top speed.
- 10 thrusters fed by 600 L of compressed air in COPVs, rechargeable.
- Musk floated jumping Snake River Canyon, said "safety is not the main goal", and later posted that the car will be "sentient" and do the tricks on its own.
- Numbers that don't line up: one X story summary cites 0-60 in 0.55 s with thrusters. MKBHD posted "half the speed of sound (380+mph)". Tesla's own page says 230+ mph. Don't repeat any of these figures in our ads.

**Reality check.** It's a thruster-assisted *hop*, not sustained flight. The flying version isn't street legal and is invite-only. The Verge headline: "just for show."
