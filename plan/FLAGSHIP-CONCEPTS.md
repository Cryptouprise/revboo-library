## Concept 2: "Planet Ad" (cross-industry arbitrage: the nature documentary)

- **Hook:** "Here, in the wild, an ad has been running for 94 days." *(Note: "94 days" is a story detail, not a stat. Swap it for "far too long" if that's too close to a number.)*
- **Length:** 45 s (9:16 + 4:5).
- **Why it works:** *format transplant*. Prestige-nature-doc grammar (hushed narrator, slow-motion, a predator) on a boring business topic is an instant pattern interrupt, and people share it because it's funny. It dramatizes **creative fatigue** without a single number. The ad is the "animal", so nobody feels attacked. *Empathy, then relief*: the old ad "dies", and a fresh herd arrives.

**First 3 seconds:** golden-hour savanna. A lone, tired **static banner ad** (a printed BIG SALE poster on stilts, like a giraffe) stands in tall grass. A hushed Adam: "Here, in the wild… an ad has been running for ninety-four days." Caption: "Ninety-four *days*."

## Concept 3: "Monday Night Creative" (arbitrage: the live sports broadcast)

- **Hook:** "Welcome to the only league where the ads play each other."
- **Length:** 60 s (9:16 + 16:9).
- **Why it works:** *the sports-broadcast frame turns A/B testing into a contest with a winner*. Stakes, rooting interest and suspense are built in. Two commentators (Adam play-by-play, Jessica color) make "testing velocity" exciting and easy to grasp. Instant replay + telestrator is a perfect excuse for **live teardowns**. A *variable reward*: viewers guess the winner before the reveal, which drives comments.

**First 3 seconds:** a stadium flyover at night. The jumbotron shows two ads facing off ("HOOK A vs HOOK B"). Adam, booming: "Welcome to the only league where the ads play each other!" Caption: "Ads play each *other*."

## Concept 4: "One Line, Every Genre" (original: the range flex)

- **Hook:** "We gave the same line to eight different directors."
- **Length:** 30 s (9:16 + 4:5). Doubles as the hook bank for The Set.
- **Why it works:** *repetition with variation*. The ear locks onto one repeated line, so the eye gets to enjoy the genre changes (a fast pattern interrupt every 3 s keeps the retention curve flat). It **proves range in 30 s** and makes the brand line unforgettable through repetition. It's highly *saveable/shareable* ("which one's your favorite?"), and the comments become a vote.

**First 3 seconds:** a UGC selfie. The Owner in his car looks straight into his phone: "Your ads deserve better creative." HARD CUT on the word "creative" to the same line in anime. Caption: "Same line. Eight *directors*."
